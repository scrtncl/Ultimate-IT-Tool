Network Terms Explained

What the numbers and diagnoses mean in plain language

Routing

Default Gateway

The "front door" of your local network. It is usually your main router (or your phone when tethering). All traffic destined for the outside internet passes through this address.

Example: 192.168.100.1 (home router) or 10.141.94.14 (hotspot).
Name Resolution

DNS (Domain Name System)

The internet's phonebook. When you type "example.com", DNS translates that friendly name into the numerical IP address the computer needs to connect.

If slow: Websites take seconds before they even begin loading.
Speed & Responsiveness

Latency & Ping (RTT)

The delay it takes for a data packet to travel from your computer to a destination and return (Round-Trip Time). Measured in milliseconds (ms). Lower is always better.

Rule of thumb: Under 20 ms is excellent for gaming/calls; over 100 ms feels laggy.
Reliability

Packet Loss

When pieces of data sent across the network get lost and never arrive. Even a small loss (1–2%) can cause video calls to freeze, games to stutter, or web pages to fail.

Target: 0% packet loss.
Responsiveness

Bufferbloat (Extra delay when busy)

When someone is downloading or uploading, unmanaged routers queue up too many packets. This causes ping times to jump dramatically even if your raw speed is fast.

What it looks like: +150 ms extra delay when downloading a file.
Wireless

2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz Wi-Fi

2.4 GHz reaches further through walls but is slower and prone to interference. 5 GHz is much faster with lower lag, but has shorter range and struggles through concrete.

Tip: Use 5 GHz in the same room; 2.4 GHz when far away.
Wireless

Wi-Fi Signal Strength (dBm & %)

Measured in negative decibel milliwatts (dBm), where numbers closer to zero mean stronger signal. Percentage is a simplified representation.

Scale: -50 dBm (90–100%) is excellent; -75 dBm (under 40%) is weak.
Privacy & Identity

Private MAC Address

Modern phones and laptops periodically generate randomized MAC addresses to prevent public trackers from following you. On local networks, this causes one phone to look like multiple devices over time.

scrtncl handling: Stale randomized addresses are safely pruned.
Security

Certificates & HTTPS

Digital security badges that prove a website is legitimate and encrypt your connection. If your computer's certificate store is outdated, scrtncl uses its built-in verified certificate list.

Safety rule: Certificate checking is never turned off in scrtncl.
Diagnostics

Ports & Port Scanning

Think of an IP address as a building, and ports as numbered room doors. Port 80/443 is web traffic, port 53 is DNS. A port scan checks which doors are open on a private device you own.

Safety guard: scrtncl only allows scanning private local devices.

The scrtncl Philosophy

How this tool is built differently

  • Read-only by default scrtncl observes, measures, and diagnoses. It never modifies your router settings or network configurations without explicit approval.
  • Privacy by default All scans, pings, diagnostics, and history stay 100% on this computer. No cloud AI services or external databases receive your IP addresses or device names.
  • Evidence-based technician Answers are strictly tied to real measurements stored in your files, separating measured facts from unknowns and possible causes.
Use this tool only on networks and routers you own or have permission to manage.
